## Clinical Implications and Expert Perspectives

Clinically, this research matters because it zones in on the important question: "How do outside variables impact the effectiveness of treatments?" When treating OCD with medication, higher doses of antidepressants are used compared to treatments for other disorders, like anxiety and depression. This means the drug is highly saturated in the patient's system. If a mineral like magnesium has the ability to form complex connections, the needed dosage for treatment could change. Patients often may use over-the-counter supplements like magnesium to deal with unwanted side effects like anxiety or insomnia. ## Future Directions

This study is just a foundation for future research. We plan to expand our computational modeling to include other biologically relevant ions (such as Ca²⁺, Zn²⁺, and Fe²⁺) and compare how drug complexation affects BBB permeability. We may also expand on which psychiatric medications to experiment with to see if they maintain similar results. Throughout our research so far, we have been met with many limitations, including restricted access to prescription compounds, limited laboratory equipment, computer power, and most of all: time constraints. These challenges can be addressed through collaboration with university laboratories, improved data collection methods, and the use of more advanced computational tools.

We will continue developing better predictive models and algorithms that support personalized treatments. Beyond the lab, we intend to submit our research to student-led research journals and present it at national science fairs. This will allow us to share our results with the broader scientific community and potentially inspire further research into personalized psychiatric treatment on the basis of biochemical variability.
